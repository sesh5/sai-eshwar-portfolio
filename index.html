About

The short version: I cannot leave a problem alone.

When I see someone fighting a clunky process or quietly working around something broken, I want to understand exactly why it hurts, and then build something that makes that pain disappear.

That curiosity is what pulls me toward product. I love sitting with people, asking the slightly annoying follow up questions, and digging until the real problem surfaces underneath the one they first described. Then comes the part I enjoy most: turning that insight into something real, an automation, a dashboard, or a working prototype I can put in front of someone the same week.

Tenacity is the other half. I would rather ship a rough first version, watch how people actually use it, and improve from there than wait for a perfect plan. Across enterprise implementations, AI tooling, and published research, the thread is the same: find a problem worth solving and build my way to the answer.

Every project below follows the same loop: find the friction, build the fix, measure what changed. Click any card for the full problem → approach → outcome story.

Problem

Swiggy and Zomato have veg filters and experimented with calorie sections, but neither lets you search the full catalog by natural-language dietary intent. The deeper blocker: restaurant menus have no public nutrition data at all, so the query "under 500 calories" has nothing to run against.

Approach

Manufactured the missing data layer with a Generate → Enrich → Load pipeline: LLM-generated restaurant catalogs with a second LLM pass estimating calories, macros, and dietary tags per dish (reconciled so macros match stated calories), loaded into Supabase Postgres. Search runs through a Supabase Edge Function: parseIntent() sends the raw sentence to Claude Haiku and gets back structured rules (max calories, min protein, exclusions), then runSearch() filters and ranks dishes against the database — the Anthropic key never touches the browser.

Outcome

The full loop works end to end: type a craving in plain English, get ranked dish cards with calories, macros, and tags, each linked to the restaurant you'd order from. Embedding-based rerank for fuzzy terms ("light", "comforting") is the next phase.

Problem

A national news network (the fictional client in a Codebasics Resume Project Challenge) needed a fact based election brief, but the raw material was candidate level ECI results scattered across two election cycles, all 234 constituencies, 8,000+ rows.

Approach

Built the full pipeline in Python: cleaned and reconciled 2021 and 2026 candidate level results, normalised party and constituency names, and computed seat flips, vote share swings, and margin distributions.

Outcome

Surfaced the headline underneath the noise: 163 of 234 seats (69.7%) flipped party and a new entrant captured 35.1% of the statewide vote. Shipped seven charts and an eleven slide stakeholder deck.

Problem

Vehicles on 5G networks exchange safety critical data on constrained hardware, heavyweight encryption is too slow, but weak security invites DoS and MITM attacks.

Approach

Deployed the ASCON lightweight cipher on Raspberry Pi boards over MQTT to simulate real time IoT encryption. Simulated DoS, MITM, and password cracking attacks with Snort and Kali Linux, then trained an ensemble ML voting classifier on CICIDS2017 for intrusion detection.

Outcome

99.95% DoS detection accuracy and ASCON validated at NPCR 99.42% against the PRESENT cipher. The work contributed to a Springer Nature publication (2025).

Problem

Stored grain spoils when temperature and CO2 drift outside safe ranges, but round the clock manual monitoring of a granary doesn't scale and losses often go unnoticed until it's too late.

Approach

Built a smart granary system that continuously tracks temperature and CO2 against defined thresholds, designed to ensure optimal storage conditions and flag timely action the moment conditions drift.

Outcome

Optimal storage conditions maintained with far less manual monitoring, reducing grain losses and protecting margins.

Problem

Poor posture builds up silently over long desk sessions, and people only notice it once the strain has already set in.

Approach

Designed and built a posture correcting device around an Arduino Nano, using accelerometer and gyroscope sensors to monitor spinal alignment in real time. Embedded C processed the sensor data to detect deviations from correct posture, compact and wearable enough for continuous, low power use.

Outcome

The device triggers a vibration motor the instant it detects improper alignment, prompting the wearer to readjust posture in the moment rather than after the damage is done.
